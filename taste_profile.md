# Taste Profile

**Status: preliminary draft, liked examples only.** Based on 3 StreetEasy
listings Elliott flagged as favorites (2026-08-22). No disliked examples
yet - this profile can only describe what these three have in common,
not what to actively score down. Treat scores from this version as
rough signal, not confident judgment. Revise `profile_version` in
`config.yaml` whenever this file's content meaningfully changes.

## The three references

1. **256 Cumberland St #4, Fort Greene** ($8,500, condo, 2bd/2bd) - "our
   favorite." High-design modern renovation: black steel casement
   windows, sage-green lower cabinets + wood upper cabinets, full
   marble-slab backsplash, wide-plank light oak floors, built-in
   white bookshelf/bar with marble counter, gas fireplace, curated
   mixed-vintage furniture (pink curved swivel chair, round wood
   coffee tables, gallery-framed art), colorful striped textiles
   against a neutral base, private terrace with skyline view.
2. **75 South 3rd St #1, Williamsburg** ($6,000, 4-family, 2bd/1ba) -
   "great too." Notably plainer/more builder-grade interior (white
   shaker kitchen, standard subway tile, generic gray sectional) -
   the real draw here is clearly the **private backyard/patio**: teak
   furniture, string lights, built-in grill, lush greenery, feels like
   a genuine outdoor room, not an afterthought.
3. **163 Warren St #1, Cobble Hill** ($5,400, 4-family, 2bd/1ba) -
   "great too." Prewar charm, not modern design: decorative ceiling
   medallions, ornate pendant lights, exposed brick fireplace wall,
   crown moldings, tall double-hung windows with good light. Currently
   occupied (visible tenant furniture, a baby's crib) - the appeal here
   is clearly the **architecture/bones** (light, ceiling detail, brick,
   window size), not the current tenant's actual furniture, which won't
   convey to a new tenant. Kitchen and bathroom are basic/dated, not a
   dealbreaker.

## What these three actually have in common

- **Good natural light and multiple/tall windows** in the main living
  space - present in all three, seems close to a hard requirement.
- **Real character, via either path**: a genuinely well-executed modern
  renovation (steel windows, marble, considered furniture) OR authentic
  prewar detail (moldings, medallions, a real fireplace, tall windows) -
  *not* a generic, characterless flip. Both count; neither is required
  over the other.
- **Private outdoor space is a strong plus, not required** - present
  and clearly a major draw in 2 of 3 (Cumberland's terrace, South 3rd's
  garden), absent (as shown) in Warren St, which still rates as "great"
  on architecture/light alone.
- **Finish level is not the deciding factor.** South 3rd's kitchen is
  plain and Warren St's bath is basic/dated - both still liked. High-end
  finishes (Cumberland) are a bonus, not a requirement.

## What this profile can't tell you yet

No disliked examples exist to contrast against, so nothing here should
be read as "always score X low." In particular, don't infer that dark
rooms, closed-off floor plans, or no outdoor space are disqualifying -
absence of a positive signal in these three examples isn't the same as
a confirmed negative. Ask for disliked examples to sharpen this.

## How to use this when scoring a listing photo

Judge the photo on: does the room look bright/well-lit with real
windows; does the space show genuine character (either well-done modern
finishes or authentic prewar architectural detail) rather than a bare,
generic flip; is there any visible private outdoor space. Ignore
whatever furniture/decor is staged or belongs to a current tenant if it
reads as personal rather than part of the unit itself (a baby's crib, a
random red sofa) - score the space, not someone else's stuff in it.
